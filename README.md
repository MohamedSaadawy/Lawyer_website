### **2022 لور أول**

**1. Illustrate the surface anatomy of the pleura and identify the best level to do thoracocentesis if indicated. (3 marks)**

**Answer:**
- The pleura consists of two layers: the **visceral pleura** (covering the lungs) and the **parietal pleura** (lining the thoracic cavity).
- Surface anatomy:
  - **Cervical pleura**: Extends above the clavicle.
  - **Costal pleura**: Lines the ribs.
  - **Diaphragmatic pleura**: Covers the diaphragm.
  - **Mediastinal pleura**: Lines the mediastinum.
- The best level for **thoracocentesis** is at the **7th or 8th intercostal space** in the midaxillary line, just above the rib to avoid neurovascular structures.

---

**2. Regarding the development of the lung:**
- Demonstrate the changes which occur during the final two stages of its development.
- Identify the possible congenital anomaly which can happen during these final stages, its mechanism, and incidence. (3 marks)

**Answer:**
- **Final two stages of lung development**:
  1. **Canalicular stage (16–26 weeks)**: Formation of respiratory bronchioles and vascularization.
  2. **Alveolar stage (26 weeks–birth)**: Formation of alveoli and maturation of surfactant production.
- **Congenital anomaly**: **Congenital diaphragmatic hernia (CDH)**.
  - **Mechanism**: Failure of the pleuroperitoneal membrane to close, allowing abdominal organs to herniate into the thoracic cavity.
  - **Incidence**: 1 in 2,500–5,000 live births.

---

**3. Describe (with diagram) Hb-Oxygen dissociation curve shape and its physiological significance. Mention causes of Rt. shift of the curve. (8 marks)**

**Answer:**
- **Shape**: Sigmoid (S-shaped) curve.
- **Physiological significance**:
  - Facilitates oxygen loading in the lungs (high PO₂) and unloading in tissues (low PO₂).
  - Cooperative binding of oxygen to hemoglobin.
- **Causes of right shift**:
  - Increased CO₂ (Bohr effect).
  - Decreased pH (acidosis).
  - Increased temperature.
  - Increased 2,3-BPG.

### **2022 لور نائى**

**I- Answer the following:**

**1. Define hypoxia? Mention causes of hypoxic hypoxia. (4 marks)**

**Answer:**
- **Hypoxia**: Deficiency of oxygen at the tissue level.
- **Causes of hypoxic hypoxia**:
  - High altitude.
  - Hypoventilation (e.g., COPD).
  - Diffusion impairment (e.g., pulmonary fibrosis).
  - Shunt (e.g., congenital heart disease).

---

**2. Describe nature, secretion, and functions of surfactant. (4 marks)**

**Answer:**
- **Nature**: A lipoprotein complex secreted by type II alveolar cells.
- **Secretion**: Stimulated by cortisol and thyroid hormones.
- **Functions**:
  - Reduces surface tension in alveoli, preventing collapse.
  - Enhances lung compliance.
  - Prevents pulmonary edema.

---

- **Right lung**:
  - **Mediastinal surface**: Heart, superior vena cava, azygos vein, esophagus.
- **Left lung**:
  - **Mediastinal surface**: Heart, aortic arch, thoracic aorta, esophagus.

---

**4. Illustrate the course and branches of typical intercostal nerves. (3 marks)**

**Answer:**
- **Course**: Run along the inferior border of each rib.
- **Branches**:
  - **Anterior cutaneous branch**: Supplies skin of the anterior chest.
  - **Lateral cutaneous branch**: Supplies skin of the lateral chest.
  - **Collateral branch**: Supplies intercostal muscles.

**Answer:**

  - Prevents alveolar collapse.
  - Enhances lung compliance.
- **Significance**: Essential for normal lung function and gas exchange.
- **Causes of deficiency**:

- **Mechanism**: Stimulated by low PO₂, high PCO₂, and low pH.

### **تدريبات فسيو**

**1. Define intrapleural pressure. Mention its value under different circumstances. Explain the causes of its negativity. (4 marks)**

**Answer:**
- **Intrapleural pressure**: Pressure within the pleural cavity.
- **Values**:
  - At rest: -5 cm H₂O (negative pressure).
  - During inspiration: -8 cm H₂O.
  - During expiration: -3 cm H₂O.
- **Causes of negativity**:
  - Elastic recoil of the lungs.
  - Chest wall expansion.

---

**2. Name the cell-secreting surfactant. Outline its functions and causes of its deficiency. (4 marks)**

**Answer:**
- **Cell**: Type II alveolar cells.
- **Functions**:
  - Reduces surface tension in alveoli.
  - Prevents alveolar collapse.
  - Enhances lung compliance.
- **Causes of deficiency**:
  - Prematurity.
  - ARDS.
  - Genetic mutations (e.g., SP-B deficiency).

---

**3. Define Dead space. Explain its physiologic significance and importance. (4 marks)**

**Answer:**
- **Dead space**: Volume of air not participating in gas exchange.
- **Types**:
  - **Anatomical dead space**: Air in conducting airways.
  - **Alveolar dead space**: Air in alveoli without perfusion.
- **Significance**:
  - Reduces efficiency of ventilation.
  - Important in calculating alveolar ventilation.

---

**4. Explain the causes of variation in ventilation-perfusion ratio in different lung regions. (4 marks)**

**Answer:**
- **Causes**:
  - Gravity: Higher perfusion at the base of the lungs.
  - Alveolar pressure: Higher ventilation at the apex.
  - Disease: Mismatch in ventilation and perfusion (e.g., COPD, pulmonary embolism).

---

**5. Mention the normal ventilation-perfusion ratio. Describe the variations in its value occurring in chronic bronchitis and in pulmonary emboli. (4 marks)**

**Answer:**
- **Normal V/Q ratio**: 0.8.
- **Chronic bronchitis**:
  - Decreased V/Q ratio (hypoventilation).
- **Pulmonary emboli**:
  - Increased V/Q ratio (dead space effect).

---

**6. Describe the factors affecting the diffusion across the pulmonary membrane. (4 marks)**

**Answer:**
- **Factors**:
  - Thickness of the membrane.
  - Surface area of the membrane.
  - Diffusion coefficient of the gas.
  - Partial pressure gradient of the gas.

---

**7. Draw a labeled diagram representing the hemoglobin-oxygen dissociation relationship. Explain the physiological significance of its S shape. (4 marks)**

**Answer:**
- **Diagram**: Sigmoid (S-shaped) curve.
- **Significance**:
  - Facilitates oxygen loading in the lungs (high PO₂) and unloading in tissues (low PO₂).
  - Cooperative binding of oxygen to hemoglobin.

---

**8. Regarding the Hb-O₂ dissociation relationship, define the P50 and discuss the factors shifting it to the right and to the left. (4 marks)**

**Answer:**
- **P50**: Partial pressure of oxygen at which hemoglobin is 50% saturated.
- **Right shift**:
  - Increased CO₂ (Bohr effect).
  - Decreased pH (acidosis).
  - Increased temperature.
  - Increased 2,3-BPG.
- **Left shift**:
  - Decreased CO₂.
  - Increased pH (alkalosis).
  - Decreased temperature.
  - Decreased 2,3-BPG.

---

**9. Define tidal CO₂. Mention how CO₂ is transported in venous blood and describe the Chloride shift phenomena. (4 marks)**

**Answer:**
- **Tidal CO₂**: Amount of CO₂ exhaled in one breath.
- **Transport**:
  - Dissolved in plasma (10%).
  - As bicarbonate (70%).
  - Bound to hemoglobin (20%).
- **Chloride shift**:
  - Exchange of chloride ions (Cl⁻) for bicarbonate (HCO₃⁻) in red blood cells to maintain electrical neutrality.

---

**10. Describe the site, stimuli, and response of central chemoreceptors to changes in blood gases. (4 marks)**

**Answer:**
- **Site**: Medulla oblongata.
- **Stimuli**: Increased PCO₂ and decreased pH in cerebrospinal fluid.
- **Response**: Increased ventilation to remove CO₂.

---

**11. Describe the site, stimuli, and response of peripheral chemoreceptors to changes in blood gases. (4 marks)**

**Answer:**
- **Site**: Carotid bodies and aortic bodies.
- **Stimuli**: Decreased PO₂, increased PCO₂, and decreased pH.
- **Response**: Increased ventilation.

---

**12. Mention the role of O₂ in regulation of respiration and explain why it is a weaker stimulus than CO₂. (4 marks)**

**Answer:**
- **Role**: Peripheral chemoreceptors respond to low PO₂.
- **Weaker stimulus**: Because CO₂ is the primary driver of respiration due to its direct effect on central chemoreceptors and pH.

---

**13. Explain the ventilatory response to changes in hydrogen concentration. (4 marks)**

**Answer:**
- **Response**: Increased hydrogen ion concentration (acidosis) stimulates peripheral and central chemoreceptors, leading to increased ventilation to remove CO₂ and normalize pH.

---

**14. Enumerate the types of hypoxia and discuss the hypoxic hypoxia (definition, causes, characters). (4 marks)**

**Answer:**
- **Types of hypoxia**:
  - Hypoxic hypoxia.
  - Anemic hypoxia.
  - Stagnant hypoxia.
  - Histotoxic hypoxia.
- **Hypoxic hypoxia**:
  - **Definition**: Low arterial PO₂.
  - **Causes**: High altitude, hypoventilation, diffusion impairment, shunt.
  - **Characters**: Cyanosis, dyspnea, impaired mental function.

---

**15. Enumerate the types of hypoxia and discuss the anemic hypoxia (definition, causes, characters). (4 marks)**

**Answer:**
- **Anemic hypoxia**:
  - **Definition**: Reduced oxygen-carrying capacity of blood.
  - **Causes**: Anemia, carbon monoxide poisoning.
  - **Characters**: Pallor, fatigue, tachycardia.

---

**16. Enumerate the types of hypoxia and discuss the stagnant hypoxia (definition, causes, characters). (4 marks)**

**Answer:**
- **Stagnant hypoxia**:
  - **Definition**: Reduced blood flow to tissues.
  - **Causes**: Heart failure, shock.
  - **Characters**: Cold extremities, low blood pressure, confusion.

---

**17. Describe the physiological responses (acclimatization) to high altitude. (4 marks)**

**Answer:**
- **Responses**:
  - Increased ventilation (hyperventilation).
  - Increased erythropoiesis (higher hemoglobin).
  - Increased 2,3-BPG (right shift of Hb-O₂ curve).
  - Renal compensation (bicarbonate excretion to correct alkalosis).

### **أسئلة تدريب مايكرو من محاضرة المراجعة**

**1. Enumerate the stages of pertussis. (2 marks)**

**Answer:**
- **Catarrhal stage**: Mild cough, cold-like symptoms.
- **Paroxysmal stage**: Severe coughing fits, whooping sound.
- **Convalescent stage**: Gradual recovery.

---

**2. Give reason, lymphocytes occur in pertussis. (2 marks)**

**Answer:**
- **Reason**: Lymphocytosis is caused by the toxin produced by *Bordetella pertussis*, which inhibits adenylate cyclase and increases lymphocyte production.

---

**3. Compare the two types of vaccine of pertussis in relation to its composition and its side effects. (4 marks)**

**Answer:**
- **Whole-cell vaccine**:
  - **Composition**: Killed *Bordetella pertussis*.
  - **Side effects**: Fever, local reactions.
- **Acellular vaccine**:
  - **Composition**: Purified antigens (e.g., pertussis toxin, filamentous hemagglutinin).
  - **Side effects**: Fewer side effects compared to whole-cell vaccine.

---

**4. Enumerate the diseases caused by Pneumococci. (2 marks)**

**Answer:**
- Pneumonia.
- Meningitis.
- Otitis media.
- Sinusitis.

---

**5. Give reason Pneumococci can escape phagocytosis. (2 marks)**

**Answer:**
- **Reason**: Presence of a polysaccharide capsule that inhibits phagocytosis.

---

**6. Explain the ability of pneumococci to colonize the URT mucosa. (2 marks)**

**Answer:**
- **Reason**: Adhesins on the bacterial surface allow attachment to respiratory epithelial cells.

---

**7. How to differentiate *Streptococcus pneumoniae* and *Streptococcus viridans* on laboratory basis. (2 marks)**

**Answer:**
- **Optochin test**: *S. pneumoniae* is sensitive, *S. viridans* is resistant.
- **Bile solubility test**: *S. pneumoniae* is soluble, *S. viridans* is insoluble.

---

**8. Give reason the capsule polysaccharide vaccine is not given to children below 2 years. (2 marks)**

**Answer:**
- **Reason**: Immature immune system cannot mount an effective response to polysaccharide antigens.

---

**9. Enumerate the factors that are needed for culturing *Haemophilus influenzae*. (2 marks)**

**Answer:**
- Chocolate agar.
- Factors V (NAD) and X (hemin).

---

**10. Compare the orthomyxo and paramyxo viruses. (4 marks)**

**Answer:**
- **Orthomyxoviruses**:
  - Segmented genome.
  - High antigenic variation (e.g., influenza).
- **Paramyxoviruses**:
  - Non-segmented genome.
  - Lower antigenic variation (e.g., RSV).

---

**11. Give reason the ability of influenza type A to cause pandemics. (2 marks)**

**Answer:**
- **Reason**: High antigenic variation due to antigenic shift and drift.

---

**12. Compare antigenic shift and antigenic drift. (4 marks)**

**Answer:**
- **Antigenic shift**:
  - Major change in viral antigens due to reassortment of RNA segments.
  - Can cause pandemics.
- **Antigenic drift**:
  - Minor changes in viral antigens due to mutations.
  - Causes seasonal epidemics.

---

**13. Explain the RNA viruses characterized by high mutation rate. (2 marks)**

**Answer:**
- **Reason**: Lack of proofreading mechanism in RNA-dependent RNA polymerase.

---

**14. Explain the name given to RSV. (2 marks)**

**Answer:**
- **Reason**: Causes syncytia formation in infected cells.

---

**15. Enumerate respiratory diseases caused by *Aspergillus fumigatus*. (2 marks)**

**Answer:**
- Allergic bronchopulmonary aspergillosis (ABPA).
- Aspergilloma.
- Invasive aspergillosis.
